Yulong WangEXTREME VALUE ECONOMETRICS

The answers are in the tails.

Associate Professor of Economics, Lehigh University
Faculty affiliate, Center for Catastrophe Modeling and Resilience

I develop econometric methods for data in which rare, extreme observations drive the results, and apply them to economic questions: international trade and factor flows, income inequality, earnings and health spending, macroeconomic and currency tail risk, auctions, and catastrophe losses. Much of my work builds inference that remains valid when only a few observations carry information about the tail and when means or variances may not exist.

Center for Catastrophe Modeling and Resilience

PREDICT · PREPARE · WITHSTAND · RECOVER

I am a faculty affiliate of Lehigh's Center for Catastrophe Modeling and Resilience, an interdisciplinary group of engineers, statisticians and economists who work with industry partners on the risk of rare, high-impact events.

Catastrophe modeling is a tail problem. The 1-in-100-year loss, the probable maximum loss and the failure probability of infrastructure in an extreme storm all sit where data are thinnest. I bring formal extreme value inference to these questions: honest uncertainty for return levels and extreme quantiles, and tail estimates that hold up when the sample contains only a handful of truly large events.

Current project: how the choice of fragility model changes hurricane risk estimates for Florida's electric power transmission network (with Xinyue Wang and Paolo Bocchini; revise and resubmit at Reliability Engineering & System Safety).

Research

Methods and applications

Heavy tails in international trade

X_ij = exp(o_i + d_j + z_ij'β) · η_ij, P(η > x) ~ x^(−α)

A few country pairs, firms and products account for a large share of trade. My recent work shows when PPML gravity estimation gives misleading standard errors under such heavy tails and how to restore valid inference. It also develops threshold methods that recover the productivity cutoffs separating exporters from non-exporters using firm profit data.

JIE 2026 · The Log of Gravity under Heavy Tails (with Ji and Egger)

Inference from a few extremes

X(1) ≥ X(2) ≥ … ≥ X(k), k fixed

Tests, confidence intervals and estimators for tail indices and extreme quantiles that stay valid when only a handful of observations are truly extreme, including conditional, censored and high-dimensional settings.

JASA 2017 · JoE 2019, 2022, 2024 · JBES 2021

When moments do not exist

E|X|^p = ∞ for p ≥ α

Diagnostic tests for finite moments in GMM and M estimation, and inference for clustered data that remains reliable when outcomes or regressors are heavy-tailed.

JBES 2023 · JAE 2026 · clustered inference (with Chiang and Sasaki)

Policy effects in the tails

Q(τ | x), τ → 1

Extreme quantile treatment effects under endogeneity and extreme changes-in-changes, for evaluating policies whose consequences show up in the far tail of an outcome.

JBES 2023, 2026

Inequality, earnings and health

P(Y > y) ≈ C·y^(−α)

Pareto exponents of top incomes estimated from tabulated shares, non-existent moments of earnings growth, and the right tail of health expenditure.

JAE 2021, 2026 · JoE 2024 · JHE 2024

Macro and financial risk, auctions, catastrophes

VaR_τ(GDP growth | conditions)

Robust econometrics for growth-at-risk, forecastable components of currency tail risk, inference in auctions with many bidders, tail behavior in stochastic frontier models, and hurricane risk to power infrastructure.

JoE 2026 · REStat fthc. · JAE 2022

Work with me

For industry and policy partners

I help teams make decisions when the outcome that matters is rare and large. Typical questions look like these.

Insurance & catastrophe risk

We have 30 years of loss data. How large is the 1-in-200-year loss, and how wide is the uncertainty?

Extreme quantile and return-level estimation with valid confidence intervals from a handful of large events.

Model validation

Does our catastrophe or credit model understate the tail?

Formal tests of tail shape and tail index against observed extremes; diagnostics for when means or variances may not exist.

Experiments & causal inference

Our metric is heavy-tailed. Are the A/B test results and standard errors still reliable?

Heavy-tail-robust inference, extreme quantile treatment effects, and surrogate outcomes for long-run effects.

Finance & AI

How do we bring sound econometric and financial reasoning into model training and evaluation?

Benchmarks, data design and evaluation grounded in econometrics, forecasting and tail risk.

Case · Workforce risk

Extreme attrition at Amazon

Applied extreme value methods to rare, high-impact attrition events and to forecasting organizational risk.

Case · Infrastructure

Hurricanes and the power grid

Assessing how fragility model choices change hurricane risk estimates for Florida's transmission network.

Case · Health spending

Getting the right tail right

Modeling the right tail of health expenditure, where a small share of patients drives a large share of cost.
